# About Remote Valley OS

**We build tools for the world's most remote places. And we give them away for free.**

---

## The Origin

In May 2026, a small team spent three weeks in **Jumla District, Karnali Province, Nepal** — one of the most remote and logistically challenging regions in the Himalayas. Accessible only by air or a multi-day road journey, Jumla concentrates every problem that remote communities face: no waste processing, unreliable electricity, contaminated water, post-harvest losses, and limited connectivity.

We walked the land. We studied the rivers, the sun, and the wind. We mapped waste streams and agricultural value chains. We talked to farmers, shopkeepers, mothers, and young people.

The conclusion was clear: remote regions are not poor in resources. They are rich in sun, water, wind, soil, and human ingenuity. What they lack is the **integration** — the systems that connect what they have to what they need — and the **training** that keeps those systems running.

We came back with a plan.

## The Philosophy

### Everything is open source

Hardware designs, software, documentation, training materials. All published under open-source licenses. Free to use. Free to modify. Free to share. No one owns it. Everyone owns it.

### Knowledge must outlast the installer

The reason most development projects fail is not bad technology. It is that knowledge leaves when the installer leaves. Our model ensures knowledge stays. Every system is built alongside local people. Every step is documented in simple, visual guides. Every operator is trained until they can fix the system blindfolded.

### Local people become the experts

Within two years, local Master Trainers run everything. The residency becomes optional. The knowledge belongs to the community.

### The blueprint bends to fit the place

This is a starting point, not a finished product. Every community that uses it should adapt it to their crops, their climate, their language. The blueprint improves with every adaptation.

## The Name

**Remote Valley OS** is named for what it is: an operating system for remote valleys. Just as an operating system manages a computer's hardware and software, RV-OS manages the systems that sustain a community — energy, waste, water, food, mobility, communication. It connects them. It monitors them. It documents them. It ensures they keep running.

The project lives as a subdomain of **Mountaineer.fi** because we believe that solving problems at the edge — where conditions are hardest, where failure has real consequences — produces solutions that work everywhere.
