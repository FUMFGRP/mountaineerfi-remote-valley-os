# Food Production — The Value-Added Agri-Product Hub

**Turn Jumla's world-class organic apples into high-value products. Stop post-harvest losses. Create year-round income for farmers.**

---

## The Problem

Jumla is famous for its organic apples. The high-altitude climate, clean air, and rich soil produce some of the best apples in Nepal — sweet, crisp, and naturally pest-resistant.

But the system is broken:

- **Post-harvest loss:** Up to 40-50% of apples rot before reaching market
- **No cold storage:** Apples spoil during transport on rough, multi-day roads
- **Price collapse at harvest:** All farmers harvest simultaneously, glutting the market
- **Middlemen capture value:** Farmers receive a fraction of the final retail price
- **No processing infrastructure:** No facility to make cider, vinegar, dried fruit, or other shelf-stable products

The result: farmers work all year for a short, low-profit season. Value that could stay in the community instead leaves it.

---

## The Solution

A **community-owned agri-processing hub** powered by the micro-grid, managed by trained local operators, and supplied by the collective of apple farmers.

The hub does four things:

| Function | Product | Shelf Life | Value Add |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold storage | Fresh apples | 3-6 months | Sell year-round at stable prices |
| Cider production | Alcoholic and non-alcoholic cider | 6-12 months | High-value beverage |
| Vinegar production | Apple cider vinegar | 2+ years | Cooking, health, cleaning |
| Dehydration | Dried apple rings, fruit leather, powder | 12+ months | Lightweight, exportable |

## The Process

### Cold Storage

```
HARVEST  →  SORT & GRADE  →  COLD STORAGE  →  PACKAGING  →  SALE
```

- Apples are harvested at peak ripeness
- Sorted by size and quality (Grade A for fresh, Grade B for processing, Grade C for juice/cider)
- Stored in a cold room (1-4°C, 90-95% humidity)
- Powered by the micro-grid (most energy-intensive in pre-cooling)
- Released to market throughout the year when prices are stable

### Cider Production

```
APPLES  →  WASH  →  CRUSH  →  PRESS  →  FERMENT  →  MATURE  →  BOTTLE  →  LABEL
```

- Apples are washed with purified water from the water module
- Crushed into pomace using a mechanical crusher
- Pressed to extract juice
- Juice is fermented with selected yeast strains (or wild fermentation for traditional style)
- Aged in stainless steel or traditional vessels
- Bottled, labelled, and sealed

**Products:**
- Fresh apple juice (pasteurized, shelf-stable)
- Hard cider (alcoholic, 4-7% ABV)
- Apple wine (higher alcohol, for aging)
- Apple brandy (distilled, after still acquisition)

### Vinegar Production

```
CIDER / JUICE  →  ACETIFICATION  →  MATURE  →  FILTER  →  BOTTLE
```

- Cider or apple juice is exposed to acetic acid bacteria (Acetobacter)
- Fermented in surface culture or submerged culture method
- Aged for 3-6 months for complex flavor
- Filtered and pasteurized
- Bottled and labeled

**Products:**
- Raw apple cider vinegar (unfiltered, "with the mother")
- Filtered apple cider vinegar (clear, for cooking)
- Flavored vinegars (herbs, chili, honey)

### Dehydration

```
APPLES  →  WASH  →  SLICE  →  TREAT (optional)  →  DEHYDRATE  →  PACKAGE
```

- Apples are washed, peeled (optional), and sliced into uniform rings or chips
- Treated with lemon juice or citric acid to prevent browning (optional)
- Dehydrated at 50-60°C using solar dehydrators or electric dehydrators powered by the micro-grid
- Dried to 15-20% moisture content
- Packaged in sealed bags with oxygen absorbers

**Products:**
- Dried apple rings
- Apple fruit leather
- Apple powder (ground dried apples for baking, baby food)
- Apple chips (thin, crispy snack)

## The Economics

### Without the Hub

| Item | Per Farmer (Season) |
|---|---|
| Harvest | 5,000 kg apples |
| Loss to spoilage | 2,000 kg (40%) |
| Sold to middleman | 3,000 kg at NPR 40/kg |
| Gross income | NPR 120,000 |
| Transport cost | -NPR 30,000 |
| **Net income** | **NPR 90,000** |

### With the Hub

| Item | Per Farmer (Season) |
|---|---|
| Harvest | 5,000 kg apples |
| Sold fresh (cold storage) | 1,500 kg at NPR 100/kg = NPR 150,000 |
| Cider/vinegar (2,000 kg) | 4,000 bottles at NPR 200 = NPR 800,000 |
| Dried apples (1,000 kg) | 200 kg dried at NPR 1,500/kg = NPR 300,000 |
| Seconds (juice, peels) | 500 kg for by-products = NPR 50,000 |
| **Gross income** | **NPR 1,300,000** |
| Hub processing fee (20%) | -NPR 260,000 |
| **Net income** | **NPR 1,040,000** |

**Result: More than 10x income per farmer.**

## Training Pipeline

### Foundation (3 months)
- Food safety basics (hygiene, contamination prevention, temperature control)
- Apple grading and quality assessment
- Cleaning and sanitation of equipment
- Basic record keeping

### Operator (6 months)
- Run cold storage (temperature management, humidity control)
- Operate crusher and press safely
- Manage cider fermentation (pitching yeast, monitoring SG, racking)
- Operate dehydrator (temperature, timing, moisture testing)
- Basic packaging and labelling

### Technician (12 months)
- Troubleshoot and repair all processing equipment
- Diagnose fermentation problems (stuck fermentation, off-flavors, infections)
- Perform vinegar quality tests (acidity, clarity)
- Calibrate pH meters and refractometers
- Train Foundation and Operator learners
- Develop new product recipes

### Master Trainer (ongoing)
- Design and set up new agri-hubs
- Develop advanced products (brandy, specialty vinegars, nutraceuticals)
- Train entrepreneurs to start their own food businesses
- Write curriculum and certifications

## Brand and Market

The hub's products carry a **Jumla Origin brand** — a story of high-altitude, organic, community-owned production.

**Target markets:**
- **Domestic:** Kathmandu, Pokhara, Nepalgunj — premium organic retail, hotels, restaurants
- **Export:** India (organic cider market), China (dried fruit), Europe/US (specialty vinegar, single-origin cider)

**Brand pillars:**
- Highest altitude apple products in the world
- 100% organic, no pesticides
- Community-owned cooperative
- Open-source production methods
- Climate-positive (renewable energy, zero waste)

## Open Source

All processing designs — crusher and press plans, fermentation protocols, dehydrator specifications, food safety procedures — are published under open-source licenses.

Any farming community anywhere can download, adapt, and build their own agri-hub. A coffee-growing region in Ethiopia can replace apples with coffee cherries. A cocoa region in Peru can process chocolate.

**The recipe is free. The knowledge is open. The value stays local.**
