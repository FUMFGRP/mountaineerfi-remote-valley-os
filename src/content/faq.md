# Frequently Asked Questions

## General

### What is Remote Valley OS?

Remote Valley OS is an open-source blueprint for building circular economies in remote, resource-constrained regions. It includes designs for renewable energy, waste-to-manufacturing, water purification, food processing, electric transport, and mesh communication — plus a residency program that transfers knowledge permanently to local communities.

### Is this a charity project?

No. It is a construction project. We are not asking for pity for remote villages. We are providing tools and training so that communities can build and maintain their own infrastructure.

### Why Jumla?

Jumla is one of the hardest places in Nepal — high altitude, terrible roads, extreme isolation. If the blueprint works there, it can work anywhere. Jumla is the pilot, not the destination.

### Who is behind this?

Remote Valley OS is a project of [Mountaineer.fi](https://mountaineer.fi). The team includes engineers, designers, educators, and community development practitioners based in Nepal, Finland, and beyond.

## Open Source

### What does "open source" mean here?

All hardware designs, software, firmware, documentation, and training materials are published under open-source licenses. This means:

- Anyone can download and use them for free
- Anyone can modify and improve them
- Anyone can share them
- No licensing fees, no vendor lock-in, no permission needed

### What licenses do you use?

- **Hardware:** [CERN Open Hardware License (CERN OHL-S)](https://ohwr.org/cern_ohl_s_v2.txt)
- **Software:** [GNU Affero General Public License (AGPL-3.0)](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html)
- **Documentation:** [Creative Commons BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)

### Can I use this for my own community?

Yes. That is the entire point. Download the plans, adapt them to your local conditions, and start building. You do not need our permission.

### Do I have to share back my improvements?

You are not legally required to, but we strongly encourage it. When you share back, every other community benefits. The Innovation Board grows stronger for everyone.

## Residency Program

### Who can apply?

Engineers, makers, scientists, educators, and builders of all kinds. Specific tracks include renewable energy, mechanical engineering, 3D printing, food science, water sanitation, curriculum design, and general construction.

### How long is the residency?

Minimum 3 months. Preferred 6-12 months. The most impactful work happens over a full agricultural or seasonal cycle.

### What is provided?

Free housing in a natural home powered by clean energy, a modest food stipend, and access to the fabrication lab and tools.

### What is expected?

At least 50% of your working time applied to a defined project. You must document everything, train at least three local operators until they pass blind troubleshooting, and share your work under open-source licenses.

### How do I apply?

Email **residency@mountaineer.fi** with your background, a project idea, your preferred duration, and any questions.

## Remote Contribution

### I cannot travel to Nepal. Can I still help?

Yes. The [Innovation Accelerator](join.md) is designed for remote contributors. You can design components, write documentation, translate guides, analyze data, develop business models, or adapt the blueprint for a new region.

### How much time do I need to commit?

No minimum. Contribute for an hour or a year. Every contribution is credited.

### How do I start?

Visit [github.com/regenerative-valley-os](https://github.com/regenerative-valley-os) and look for issues labeled "good first issue" or browse the `rv-accelerator` repository.

## The Systems

### How do you turn plastic waste into filament?

Community-collected plastic is sorted by type, shredded into flakes, melted, and extruded through a precision nozzle to create consistent 3D printer filament. That filament feeds a printer farm that makes spare parts, tools, and other items on demand.

### How does the micro-grid work?

It combines solar panels, a run-of-river hydro turbine, and small wind turbines with lithium iron phosphate battery storage. The system provides 24/7 electricity without diesel, even during winter when hydro output is low.

### Why charging stations every 50km?

50km matches the practical range of electric two- and three-wheelers in mountainous terrain. Each station is also a water purification point and community hub, maximizing the value of each location.

### What is eVTOL?

Electric Vertical Takeoff and Landing aircraft. They need no runway — just a small clearing. We use them for medical evacuations, urgent parts delivery, and transporting high-value products to market.

### Do you have working hardware yet?

The field study is complete and the designs are published. Phase 1 construction begins in Jumla in 2026-2027. If you want to contribute to building the first systems, [apply for the residency](join.md).

## Privacy & Ethics

### How do you ensure the community actually benefits?

The collective owns the processing hub. Farmers set prices. Local Master Trainers run the systems. The residency is designed to make itself unnecessary within two years. We do not extract profit — everything is reinvested in the community or published as open source.

### How is local knowledge respected?

Every resident works alongside local counterparts. Local knowledge informs every design decision. Documentation is translated into Nepali and the local dialect. Elders and traditional practitioners are consulted.
