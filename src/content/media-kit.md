## The One-Paragraph Summary

Remote Valley OS is an open-source collection of five interconnected infrastructure systems designed for remote communities: waste-to-filament 3D printing, water purification, hybrid solar-hydro energy microgrid, value-added food production, and electric vehicle logistics. All systems were field-tested during a three-week pilot in Jumla, Nepal (May 2026). Everything is freely available under open-source licenses — hardware designs, software, and documentation. A residency program invites people to build alongside local communities, and a remote accelerator lets anyone contribute from anywhere.

## The Story Angles

### Angle 1: Open source meets the developing world
Open source transformed software. Now it is transforming hardware and infrastructure. Remote Valley OS applies the open-source model to the physical systems that remote communities need — and proves it works in one of the most challenging environments on Earth.

### Angle 2: From plastic waste to 3D printer filament
The trash-to-filament module turns a problem (plastic waste piling up in communities with no waste management) into a resource (3D printer filament for making replacement parts, tools, and educational materials).

### Angle 3: A residency in the Himalayas
The residency program invites engineers, designers, and community organizers to live in Jumla for 3-12 months, building and maintaining infrastructure alongside local residents. It is part Peace Corps, part open-source hackathon.

### Angle 4: The local LLM running on a microgrid
One of the most unexpected components: a locally-hosted large language model running on the village microgrid, providing AI-powered assistance for maintenance, education, and healthcare — completely offline.

### Angle 5: The EV corridor in the mountains
An electric vehicle corridor spanning 50km in one of the most rugged landscapes on the planet. Charging stations powered entirely by renewable microgrids. Cargo eVTOL drones for emergency supply delivery.

### Angle 6: Field-validated, not theoretical
Many development projects produce reports. This one produced working hardware. The three-week Jumla pilot tested every system in real conditions. The designs reflect real feedback from the community that will use them.

## FAQ for Journalists

**Q: Is this a charity?**
A: No. It is an open-source project. Mountaineer.fi is a for-profit entity, but all hardware, software, and documentation produced under Remote Valley OS is released under open-source licenses. The goal is to create a self-sustaining ecosystem, not a perpetual aid project.

**Q: How is it funded?**
A: Currently self-funded by Mountaineer.fi. Future funding sources include grants, partnerships with development organizations, and the parallel ShilajitBrand business that supports local payouts and infrastructure maintenance.

**Q: Is anyone actually using this?**
A: The systems were tested in Jumla during a 3-week pilot in May 2026. Local community members were trained as operators. The systems are designed to be operated and maintained by the community with minimal external support.

**Q: Could this work outside Nepal?**
A: Yes. Each module is designed to be adapted to local conditions. The innovation-board model is a template for adaptation to other regions. The rv-adaptations repository encourages and documents regional variants.

**Q: Is it really free?**
A: Yes. Every design file, BOM, firmware source, and documentation page is available on GitHub. No registration required. No proprietary software needed. The only cost is the physical materials to build the hardware.
